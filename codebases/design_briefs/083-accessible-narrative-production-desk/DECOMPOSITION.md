# Accessible Narrative Production Desk

## Audience knowledge over an editable media timeline

Accessibility work uses stable scene identity, actual timing windows and what the audience may know at each moment. A visible clue may be described without revealing its later significance. Captions, audio description and transcript cooperate to preserve essential information rather than independently repeating every source detail.

A source edit retimes only dependent segments. NatLang chooses information/channel tradeoffs and escalates scenes with no usable description gap to an approved alternative channel or edit. Exact interval arithmetic is a small helper. Speaker identity, inferred motive and narrative revelation have separate evidence checks. Release requires a coherent media revision and actual target-format/player validation receipts, not only locally plausible cue text.

The original domain records, five semantic gates, five operational scenarios, and independent finite gate answers remain the contract. Public operations and checkpoints belong to this application. Native loading and source-call inspection do not prove learned execution quality. Effect intent is distinct from an authorized receiver receipt.

The public operation constructs its complete typed carried state on every return, including blocked and no-change outcomes. Only the domain-specific observed change updates its own fields; prior receipts, unrelated history and unresolved external outcomes remain inspectable. Guardrail findings are consumed by the result or the action eligibility they govern.
