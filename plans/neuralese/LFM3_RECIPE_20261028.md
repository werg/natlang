## Data curation (starting allocation; adjusted from an audit of what the sources contain)

35% semantic delegation (awaited per-item `nl`, aggregation in code, multiple criteria, ambiguous and negative
cases), 20% exact computation (sort/filter/count/join/paging/date boundaries in eval), 15% structural iteration
(`iterateOn`, stopping conditions, stateful services, effect verification), 15% scope and child behaviour (inputs
and services used directly, correct function resolution, declared-type answers, no self-calls), 15% broader verified
natlang (editing, tool use) for breadth. Neuralese examples span these categories. Every selected trajectory has a
verified root outcome and correct intermediate actions; a successful root does not make an invalid child action a
target. No additional teacher generation (owner 2026-10-28): prioritize existing sources by how well they demonstrate the target idioms; gaps are covered by RL/JOLT+ with exact oracles. Generic
third-party coding data stays outside this recipe (breadth comes from natlang domains).

Idiom audit of admitted teacher sources (B, 2026-10-28; 11 sources, 39,777 decisions, 17,651 evals): `nl` in 24.8% of
evals (40.7% in criteria/workbench, none in knowledge/QA and workflow sources), 66% of `nl` calls awaited (un-awaited
4.4% of evals: exclude or repair), per-item `nl` a minority even in criteria shapes; `iterateOn` 2.7% (420 of 470 from
one static iteration source and actor_greenhouse; actor_route_replan none); `.sort()` 4.2% (60.8% in relational);
`finish:true` 28.5%; zero input/service redeclarations. Consequence: structural iteration and awaited per-item delegation come from oracle-built training-split tasks and JOLT+ RL (no teacher generation, owner 2026-10-28).

## Objective

Assistant actions and typed answers dominate. Reasoning gets a small separately normalized share (start 10% of
assistant loss, measure). Prompts, instructions, inputs and tool/service outputs are context with zero loss weight
(owner 2026-10-28, whole-trajectory supervision retired). Failed
historical actions get zero positive weight. Balance by invocation and task family. Use naturally concise verified
demonstrations; never truncate reasoning or strip it where later actions depend on it.

## Parameters and schedule

Start from the instruction-tuned LFM3 with its chat template and tool serialization. First stage trains the new
Neuralese parameters plus adapters; widen to the backbone as soon as development evidence shows the adapter limits
learning (no anchor caps learning; base-relative KL is telemetry). Learning rates are chosen from actual parameter
updates and behaviour. Optimizer/RNG/exposure are preserved across every continuation.

## Optimizer and precision (owner, 2026-10-28: Muon, no full precision, QAT to NVFP4/FP8)

Muon for every hidden 2-D weight matrix of the LFM3 backbone (attention q/k/v/o and the per-head gate projection,
feed_forward w1/w2/w3, conv in_proj/out_proj): Nesterov 0.95, 5 Newton-Schulz steps, update scaled 0.2*sqrt(max(m,n))
so lr is on AdamW's scale, decoupled weight decay (Mellum2's settings). AdamW-family for what Muon does not apply to:
tied embedding/output rows, the learned sparse Neuralese/reasoning rows and the low-rank embedding-space adapter
(AGENTS.md "Native optimizer routing"), RMSNorm gains and the depthwise conv kernels ([d,1,7]).

No full precision (owner): parameters and optimizer state are BF16 everywhere, including the sparse marker/reasoning
rows, with stochastic-rounding writes (`MuonSR` for matrices; a stochastic-rounding Adam-family path for the rest).
The earlier BF16 sparse-row failure was lost round-to-nearest updates, so per-row update norms and changed
coordinates stay monitored. DiLoCo keeps Muon as the inner optimizer. Exception (owner, 2026-10-28): the CPU DiLoCo coordinator keeps its global weights, outer momentum and global-version EMA in FP32 (no GPU memory cost; small merged pseudo-gradients would round away in BF16); learners stay BF16.

QAT target (owner): NVFP4 and FP8, matching Liquid's own LFM3 releases so their checkpoints are direct comparisons
and the deployment formats are what SGLang/vLLM/TensorRT load:
- **NVFP4 point** (`LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B-NVFP4@b30025bb`, ModelOpt): feed_forward w1/w2/w3 only, weights and input
  activations FP4 E2M1, group 16, FP8 E4M3 group scales plus a per-tensor FP32 scale, static activation scales;
  attention, conv, embeddings and head unquantized.
- **FP8 point** (`LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B-FP8@243955da`, compressed-tensors FP8_BLOCK): attention q/k/v/o and
  feed_forward weights E4M3 with 128x128 block scales, input activations dynamic E4M3 per group of 128; conv,
  attention gate_proj, embeddings and head unquantized.
Both are gated points of the shared quantization component (BF16 latents + fake quant with straight-through
gradients, ramped over curriculum progress, multi-precision loss); FP8 ramps first, NVFP4 is the required deploy
point. Each stage qualifies at BF16, FP8 and NVFP4 on the development packet; Liquid's own NVFP4/FP8 checkpoints
are measured as base references.

## Schedule, learning rate and telemetry (from TRAINING_INSIGHTS_20261028.md)

Constant learning rate after warmup with an EMA of the weights (BF16, stochastic-rounding updates, checkpointed);
evaluation and selection read the EMA; release/qualification checkpoints come from a short anneal branch (+EMA)
off the live state, so the main lineage never commits to an end step. Learning rate tuned once at the local LFM3
geometry and transferred with the Muon rules (sqrt(batch ratio) for 2-4x, fixed lr + higher weight decay beyond
8x, ((1-m')/(1-m))^0.6 for momentum), then confirmed on the development packet. Normal per-matrix Muon (LFM3 is
standard GQA with QK-norm). Telemetry: per-matrix norm growth and max attention logit, including at Neuralese
consumer positions.

Implementation (shared native SFT trainer, both lines; every option absent = the old behaviour bit for bit):
- `--weight-ema '{"decay":0.999,"warmup":10,"eval":"raw|ema|both","seed":0}'` (`train/weight_ema.py`). The EMA lives in
  each trainable parameter's dtype (BF16 latents give a BF16 EMA), is updated after every committed optimizer update
  with `optim.stochastic_round_` driven by its own `torch.Generator` (state saved in the EMA state), so the global RNG
  and the trained weights are unchanged by enabling it. It is saved as `training['ema']` in the full-state checkpoint,
  restored exactly on resume and on `--continue-from` (decay/warmup may be re-declared; `--weight-ema off` discards a
  carried EMA explicitly, omitting the option over a carried EMA is an error), and earns no step, exposure or epoch
  credit. `eval: ema` runs held evaluation (and so best-checkpoint selection) on the EMA weights by swapping them in
  and out; `both` also records `metrics.held_ema`. Not qualified with an FP8 training backend (export instead), task
  eval stays on the raw weights. `python -m natlang_neuralese.train.weight_ema export --checkpoint C --out D` writes a
  model-only EMA checkpoint (never a resumable parent). DiLoCo: a learner keeps no local EMA; the coordinator keeps an
  FP32 EMA of the global versions (`native_diloco init --ema '{"decay":0.99,"warmup":0}'`, merged in the same atomic
  commit as each fragment version) and `native_diloco export --ema-out DIR` writes it as a model-only checkpoint.
- Anneal branch: `python -m natlang_neuralese.train.lr_anneal run --from LIVE_CKPT --out BRANCH_DIR --steps N
  [--shape linear|cosine] [--floor F] [-- extra trainer args]` copies the live checkpoint (identity-checked), rebuilds
  the trainer arguments from the options the checkpoint recorded (production publication, resident adoption, DiLoCo and
  one-shot migrations dropped), runs `--lr-anneal` for N updates into `BRANCH_DIR/branch` and exports the continued EMA
  to `BRANCH_DIR/release-candidate-ema`. The main lineage directory is never written; `anneal-provenance.json` and the
  branch checkpoint options (`lr_anneal`, `adopt_as_lineage_parent: false`) carry the provenance.
- `--matrix-telemetry '{"every":100,"norms":true,"attention_logits":true,"layers":null}'` (`train/matrix_telemetry.py`):
  per-matrix Frobenius norm and ratio to the lineage's reference norms (first telemetry-enabled parent, kept in the
  checkpoint options) for every matrix kind by module path (q/k/v/o/gate_proj, feed_forward.w1-w3, conv.in/out_proj,
  embeddings), and the max pre-softmax attention logit per attention layer measured inside the real training forward
  (`model/attention_probe.py`; one extra QK product per observed layer on logged steps, `layers: N` samples N evenly
  spaced layers) over all visible pairs, imported Neuralese keys, and consumer rows. Written to
  `matrix-telemetry.jsonl`; a compact view rides in `progress.json`.

## RL objective

Score-centered policy gradient (Marek & Ryabinin, 2609.20807) as the shared whole-graph RL loss: per sampled
token, -R (sg[w_y] log p_y - sum over the sampler's logged top-k of sg[q_v w_v - alpha p_v] log p_v), k = 32-128,
plain (w = 1) for fresh rollouts and composed with truncated IS for stale ones; group-centered advantages. Actors
log top-k log-probs at every sampled position, which lets rollouts come from the deployed NVFP4/FP8 policy while
the learner trains BF16 latents.

Implemented as the declared grpo policy loss `{"schema": "score-centered/1", "k": 128, "importance":
"none"|"tis"|"mis", "eps": 1e-6}` (`rl/policy_math.py`), also the outcome estimator inside JOLT+ (below). Selected by
recipe stage parameter `rl_policy_loss`, RL controller config `policy_loss` (`production configure --rl-policy-loss`,
round declarations `policy_loss`), `native_stage --rl-policy-loss`, or a semantic `policy_schedule.policy_loss`. New
controller configs default to JOLT+ (coefficient/semantic actors: score-centered/1); `{"schema": "clipped-ratio/1"}`
is the explicit opt-out and keeps the historical clipped objective bytes. Captured HTTP rollouts record each sampled
position's top-k of the exact temperature/mask law by default (k = 128, `natlang.behavior-top-k/1`: int32 IDs + fp16
log-probs, ~1.06 kB JSON per sampled token, 0.29 kB at k = 32; `x_natlang_rl_top_k: 0` opts out). The declaration is
bound in the objective, checkpoint objective binding, collection binding, active-round journal and completed-update
record; historical rounds without one keep the clipped ratio loss.

Implementation (2026-10-28, `train/qformats.py`, `train/quantization.py`, `export/lfm3_quantized.py`):
- Formats `nvfp4` and `fp8_block` are exact codecs of ModelOpt NVFP4 and compressed-tensors FP8_BLOCK. NVFP4 uses a
  global scale amax/(6·448), E4M3 group scales amax/(6·s2) (a zero scale is stored as 1), and E2M1 rounding to nearest
  with ties to the even code, saturating, with the sign kept on values that round to zero. w1/w3 share one global
  scale (`shared_global`), because engines fuse gate/up into one GEMM. FP8_BLOCK uses scale amax/448 computed in FP32
  and stored in BF16, and codes E4M3 of W/s with the division in BF16. Activations use `fp8_group`, which is dynamic
  per token and per 128 values: an FP32 scale max(amax, 1e-10)/448, following vLLM's `per_token_group_quant_fp8`. NVFP4
  inputs use a static `input_scale` with dynamic E4M3 group scales that saturate at 448. Both quantizers are
  straight-through and are ramped by the point's λ, as weights are. `names` selects modules inside a `classify` group,
  for example attention without `gate_proj`.
- Exactness: quantizing the BF16 base `LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B@40ebe0aa` with these codecs reproduces Liquid's checkpoints
  bit for bit. For NVFP4 (`@b30025bb`) this covers all 72 FFN modules: codes, group scales and global scales.
  `export --input-scales-from` therefore writes all 502 of their tensors identically, and `quantization_config` and
  `hf_quant_config.json` are equal. For FP8 (`@243955da`) this covers all 116 modules: codes and block scales, so all
  402 tensors are identical and `quantization_config` is equal. Two sources of mismatch were found and fixed: w1/w3
  NVFP4 global-scale sharing (before the fix, 91-95% of codes matched on the affected modules), and computing the FP8
  scale clamp in FP32 rather than with BF16 eps. FP32 division instead of BF16 division would match only 97.0% of FP8
  codes. The only config difference is an extra `initializer_range: 0.02` in Liquid's NVFP4 `config.json`.
- Calibration of the static NVFP4 input scales follows ModelOpt `max`: the maximum |input| over BF16 forwards of the
  current latents. The trainer calls `Quantization.calibrate(forward, batches, step)` over its next `batches` training
  batches whenever `calibration_due(step)` holds, which means before the first update and then every `refresh` updates.
  The ranges are trainer state (`state_dict`/`load_state_dict`, saved with the optimizer). They are fixed between
  refreshes, so out-of-range inputs saturate as they would when deployed, and they are exported as `input_scale`. Each
  of Liquid's input scales equals float32(BF16 amax / 2688), which is consistent with `max` calibration on BF16
  activations; their calibration data is unknown. Our 8×1024-token calibration on plan text gave amax between 0.26×
  and 1.53× theirs (median 0.81). KL to BF16 on 8 held windows of the same text, at λ = 1 on the reference model:
  FP8 0.021, NVFP4 0.148 with our scales, NVFP4 0.138 with Liquid's scales. CE was 4.053 for BF16, 4.058 for FP8,
  4.079 for NVFP4 with our scales and 4.115 with theirs.
- Gates: `gate_columns` evaluates each gated point at λ = 1 with weights and activations quantized, and refuses a
  point with uncalibrated static scales.
- Real low-precision GEMMs: on torch 2.11+cu130 on GB10 (sm_121), the probe `scripts/probe_lowp_matmul.py` ran a
  4096³ GEMM, compared against BF16 at 2.02 ms. NVFP4 through `F.scaled_mm` (BlockWise1x16 + TensorWise, swizzled
  scales) ran at 0.45 ms, 4.5× faster. FP8 rowwise through `_scaled_mm` ran at 0.98 ms (2.1×) and FP8 tensorwise at
  1.37 ms (1.5×). FP8 1x128/128x128 block scaling, which is what FP8_BLOCK deploys, is not available: it is SM90-only
  in torch, and cuBLAS returns NOT_SUPPORTED. QAT therefore stays fake-quant; no kernels have been written yet.

Sample component (stage ids are placeholders until the LFM3 recipe JSON names its stages; the points are

## RL default: JOLT+ self-teaching (owner, 2026-10-28; TRAINING_INSIGHTS_20261028.md section 7)

JOLT+ is the default RL objective. The same LFM3 policy scores student rollouts with privileged training-world context
(reference solutions, oracle checks, earlier-attempt feedback) as a dense on-policy reverse-KL target, the teacher role
is trained toward student-followable guidance, and the outcome terms use the score-centered estimator. Development
measurements tune beta_S/beta_T; they do not gate adoption.

Implementation (`rl/jolt.py`, declaration `{"schema": "jolt-plus/1", "beta_s": 1.0, "beta_t": 0.5, "outcome":
<score-centered/1, k = 128>}`): blocks, custody and graph edits are the shared `rl/privileged_context.py` /
`rl/privileged_scoring.py` mechanism. Each admitted episode is packed with a stop-gradient twin of its whole graph
(`teacher_form` for a student episode, `student_form` for a teacher attempt, whose blocks the learner rebuilds and
verifies token for token); the twin rows equal `teacher_scores`. Per sampled position: -A U(score-centered) +
beta KL(p || sg p~) with the KL from logit differences (`reverse_kl`), i.e. J_OPSD (beta_S) for students and the
reward-consistent teacher KL (beta_T) for teacher attempts; A is the return minus its (task, group, role) mean, and
groups without spread still contribute the dense term. beta_S = 1 puts the per-token reverse KL (typically 0.05-1
nat) on the scale of the outcome term; beta_T = 0.5 bounds the teacher's tilt pi_S exp(A/beta_T) to about e^2 at
|A| = 1. A custody refusal or unsupported graph leaves a student episode on its outcome term (recorded per row and in
the `jolt` metrics: per-term contributions, privileged/unprivileged episode counts, mean KL per role). Teacher
attempts are served with `x_natlang_privileged_block` (`{insert_at, token_ids}`, captured as a `privileged`
segment). Open: teacher-attempt collection in the RL controller, twins for coefficient (per-invocation actor)
graphs, semantic-codec graphs (refused like custody), and the shared stable-softmax module (`reverse_kl` and
`score_centering_weights` are the switch points).
