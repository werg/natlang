# Restore a recovered historical communication declaration

The production policy keeps the original finite communication foundation while
new communication and ordinary data remain mandatory. A temporary recovery run
may disable foundation scheduling because its header was unavailable. That
switch must not force a different foundation after the exact header is recovered.

1. Supply an explicit recipe-bound recovery document. It binds the historical
   recipe, exported training metadata and full-checkpoint verification receipt.
2. Verify all referenced bytes. The historical checkpoint must be an actual
   ancestor of the loaded checkpoint, and its model/tokenizer/stage must agree.
3. Verify that the historical recipe and saved training options declared this
   exact recovered foundation. Every recovered graph must still match both the
   active train source and the saved stage's row bindings.
4. Restore only permission to resume that declaration. Preserve all weights,
   moments, RNG, quotas, pending obligations and actual exposure. Record the
   recovery document in saved options without granting qualification.
5. The normal trainer independently calculates actual foundation exposure and
   runs its runtime, gradient, held and task checks. Normal initial-cohort checks
   remain mandatory when no valid recovery declaration exists.

This is exact recovery plumbing, not a new curriculum or model-facing policy.
Both students use the same verifier and recipe role. No historical reader is
silently replaced: the old recipe is authenticated as a declaration document,
not executed through today's recipe loader.

Recovery publication retains the selected foundation header, its actual payloads
and every consulted staged-source identity map. The provider checks this exact
inventory for active and carried stage frames, including completed frames;
unselected stale declarations are not current recovery dependencies. The recovery
proof retains its directly authenticated historical documents without traversing
obsolete checkpoint paths mentioned inside those documents. These are shared
exact custody and durability helpers, not additional training policy.
