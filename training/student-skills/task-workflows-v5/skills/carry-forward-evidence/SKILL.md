# Carry forward evidence across steps

Keep the information needed by the next step's actual output contract available as work moves between steps, notes, and helpers. A supported compact judgment or typed predicate can be enough when it faithfully records the required result; do not require facts to be repeated when the contract does not need them.

- Preserve decision-relevant facts, including the entity they describe, dates or deadlines, units and thresholds, scope, and where each fact came from when those details are needed for the next decision or output. Keep enough support to justify any conclusion that is carried forward.
- Distinguish current facts and conclusions from historical ones. When new evidence resolves an uncertainty or supersedes a conclusion, update the current statement in place. Retain the earlier statement only when useful, clearly labeled as historical, and keep the evidence that supports the update.
- Match the output to its purpose. If later work needs an accumulated evidence record, do not replace that record with only a final decision, a status label, or a short summary. A summary is useful when it retains the facts and qualifications needed downstream.
- When delegating, pass the current evidence, the criterion or rule to apply, and the requested output. If the next helper must assess underlying facts, include the facts needed for that assessment in its explicit inputs. If a prior helper already produced a supported result under the same criterion and the next step only needs that result, pass the result and its scope rather than needlessly repeating the full evidence.
- Check helper conclusions against the supplied evidence and criterion. If a helper's statement conflicts with supported facts, correct it using those facts and carry the correction forward. Do not repeat a conclusion that lacks support. Keep a cause unresolved only when the available evidence does not establish it.
- Preserve distinctions among established, contradicted, and unknown facts. Do not turn an unknown cause into an extra condition, or treat a historical uncertainty as current after evidence has resolved it.

Use concise notes where possible, but retain enough source-grounded detail for the next step to reproduce the decision.
